Finance group project · Risk of government bonds

Are government bonds always “safe”?

Real bond returns in the inflation surge of 2021–23.

    What “safe” means

    Why they are called safe

    • Low default riskA financially strong government is unlikely to miss a payment: it can raise revenue through taxes.
    • High liquidityMajor government bonds trade all the time, so a seller can usually find a buyer quickly.

    “Safe” can mean three things

    1. 1
      Receiving the promised paymentsInterest and principal arrive in full and on time.
      Strong point
    2. 2
      Preserving the market valueThe price at which the bond can be sold today.
      Not guaranteed
    3. 3
      Maintaining purchasing powerWhat the money received can actually buy.
      Not guaranteed

    The risks that remain

    Low default risk is not risk-free

    A bond can be safe in one sense and risky in another.

    Four risks for the investor

    • In focus todayInterest-rate riskWhen market rates rise, existing fixed-rate bonds fall in price. Selling before maturity can mean a capital loss.
    • In focus todayInflation riskEvery payment arrives as promised, but the money buys less than expected.
    • Liquidity riskIn periods of market stress, selling quickly can become harder or mean a worse price.
    • Currency riskFor a bond in a foreign currency, exchange-rate changes affect the return in the investor’s own currency.

    Inflation risk

    What counts is the real return

    • Nominal returnBefore inflation: coupon income plus any change in price.
    • Real returnAfter inflation: the change in purchasing power.
    Real return= 1 + nominal return1 + inflation rate − 1

    Protection

    A hedge: inflation-indexed bonds

    • The principal follows pricesA US TIPS adjusts its principal with inflation.
    • The coupon is paid on that principalThe rate is fixed; the dollars paid rise with prices.
    • The trade-offA lower coupon rate than an ordinary bond, and lower payments if prices fall.

    Conclusion

    Safe from what?

    Question 1

    Safe from default?

    Tap to reveal
    Default risk

    Yes

    Every promised payment was made, in full and on time.

    Question 2

    Safe from inflation?

    Tap to reveal
    Inflation risk

    No

    A 1.125% coupon against 9.1% inflation is a real return of −7.31%. A TIPS would have kept its purchasing power.

    Question 3

    Safe if you sell early?

    Tap to reveal
    Interest-rate risk

    No

    Higher rates lowered market prices. A TIPS is exposed too when real yields rise.

    TIPS protect against inflation, but that does not make them free from every risk.
